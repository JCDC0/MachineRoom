# MachineRoom architecture

Version 0.1.401. A browser viewer that shows coding agents working on one project as a 3D machine room. It follows the room's own sources live: runs started with `run-lane` (`--lanes`), task queues on a project (S8, S8b) and the owner's Claude Code and OpenCode sessions (`--sessions`, S7a and S7b). It replays a recorded run too.

MachineRoom grew out of Paperclip: it began as its viewer and took its idea of a company of agents with tasks, budgets and approvals. Paperclip was removed on 2026-10-24, and the room now reads its own sources. S6a (0.1.233) shows lane runs live from the run-lane logs. The drawer reads tasks, live runs and costs from the lane records (`server/control.js`, `server/lane-costs.js`; L1 and L2); approvals and comments are empty, and a task, comment or agent write is refused.

## What it shows

- The page has one menu row at the top and the room in a lens viewport on the upper half. Two rails sit on the lens (`LensRail.jsx`, `src/ui/lensResize.js`): Height on its left edge at 900 pixels and wider sets the lens from 30 to 80 percent of the window (`--lens-h-user`, saved as `lensShare` in `machineroom.settings`), and at 80 percent, its default, the lens fills the window from the menu to the transport (`calc(100vh - var(--menu-h) - var(--transport-h))`), so the transport is the only display at the bottom of the viewport and the lower half waits below the fold; Zoom on its right edge moves the camera on a log scale between its nearest and furthest distance (`zoomShare`, `setZoomShare`, `zoomBy` and `zoomMultiplier` on the camera rig and the room); each has a faint label on top and a multiplier below, and follows a drag, the wheel over it and the keys. At 900 pixels and wider the whole page scrolls: the transport sits under the lens and the lower half, as tall as the window under the menu and at least 520 pixels, (`.hud-lower`, U34) works like the Antigravity app, in three columns that scroll on their own. The sidebar column is `clamp(280px, 22vw, 340px)` wide; the right column keeps its 280 pixels, or 320 with the drawer open, above 1100 pixels and narrows below that to `clamp(200px, calc(40vw - 160px), 280px)`, so the conversation takes what is left; a log line moves a path to its own line and breaks inside it only when the path is longer than the line, and its agent column fits `CLAUDE CODE`, so a path and a name stay whole; a family tree row gives its label the full first line, with the state word or task, the kind tag, `harness · model · effort`, the switch, the trash and `+` on the second line. In a live room at 900 pixels and wider the transport's middle shows `ORCHESTRATOR` with the main orchestrator's state word (`working`, `waiting for you`, `paused`), and a 22 pixel context ring with its percent sits just left of the clock: the arc fills with `contextTokens / contextWindow`, white below the autocompact rule, yellow at or past it and red past 95 percent, the ring's title is `N of WINDOW tokens`, and with no numbers it is an empty ring with `context not reported`; a click on either scrolls the page to the conversation and focuses the chat input (`src/ui/contextRing.js`, U42). With subs, a compact chip per sub follows the state word, up to four and then `+N`: the sub's name and a state dot, yellow while it works, dim when idle and red when paused or failed, from the orchestrators list Stage reads (`src/ui/transportOrchestrators.js`, U80). A click on a chip scrolls to the conversation, focuses the chat input and opens that sub's chat tab through the `machineroom:open-chat` window event with `detail: { id }`. The left column is the sidebar (`Sidebar.jsx`, U35 and U45), three tabs at its top (`SidebarTabs.jsx`) with a line icon and a label each: Chat, Projects and Agents, the open one inside a thin `#e5ff00` outline, saved as `sidebarTab` in `machineroom.settings` (`chat` by default). Chat holds a rounded outlined `+ New conversation` and the open project's conversations (pin, star, rename, delete, a read-only preview with Continue); Projects holds the current project and the recent projects that open the room on their folder; Agents holds `Build a team` (`FamilyTeam.jsx`, U81) above the family tree (`FamilyTree.jsx`, U43): it takes 1 to 4 orchestrators under the main and 1 to 3 coders each plus a harness and model for the new subs, and previews `Team 1`, `Team 2` and their lanes from the active lanes in picker order and then the idle ones, never a lane already in a sub; too few lanes says `Turn on N more lanes first.` and hides Save, and Save opens a confirm card that lists every sub and its lanes, then saves the ladder as the owner with the existing subs kept and sets each new sub's label. The family tree has the orchestrator at the root and its children indented 16 pixels a level with thin connectors, each row with its label, `harness · model · effort`, a state dot and, for a lane, an active switch and a selection checkbox; one or more checked lanes show a compact bar above the rows with `N selected`, Add sub orchestrator and Clear, and that form takes a label, harness, model and effort and saves a sub orchestrator over the checked lanes through the ladder with no new lane (U54); a click opens the node's editor, an orchestrator or a sub has Add child and a lane's `+` opens the sub form with that lane checked, a sub row and its editor carry Remove behind `Remove "LABEL"? Its agents return to the orchestrator.`, and a sub's editor lists every code agent in an Agents checklist saved through the ladder (U54); a Requests group at the top lists the agents an orchestrator proposed with Approve and Decline. The middle is the conversation: a breadcrumb `PROJECT / CONVERSATION`, the orchestrator's messages in a centred column, and the input box with the model and effort pickers, Compact with its autocompact value and rules, the context size and Stop; a replay shows the event log there instead. The right column is an Overview card whose entries, Agent and Costs, open a drawer in its place; Log and Settings open from the menu as dropdowns under it, like Control. A first live visit asks which folder to work in (`Launcher.jsx`, U36, which opens another project through the bridge, O12), then how many agents should work (`SetupCard.jsx`, U33), then runs the outlined tour. Below 900 pixels the dock stays open, the orchestrator chat floats at the bottom right, and Sessions in the folded menu opens the sidebar as an overlay.
- The middle is the hub: a flat ring where each top-level agent other than the orchestrator stays at its own slot, working or not; the orchestrator floats above the hub centre. Each lane is joined by a faint grey line to the orchestrator that leads it, its sub when the ladder puts it in one and the main otherwise, and a sub orchestrator keeps its own thin white line to the main; both are drawn under the agents and do not change while an agent works. With a ladder of sub orchestrators, the ring splits into one named arc per sector, and a sub orchestrator floats above its arc at 0.7 of the main orchestrator's size. A sector with one lane parks it at the middle of its arc, under its sub; two or more lanes sit evenly inside the arc with the same gap at each end. The sector's name outside the ring, just inside the start of its arc and a little below the ring plane, is the sub's name in capitals. `setWorking` names the agents at work: a sector with one of them brightens its arc to full white and returns it over 1 second when none works (at once under reduced motion), and the rest of the ring keeps its brightness. The room passes it the keys of the agents working in the room state, a sub orchestrator and its lanes included, and calls it only when that set changes.
- Each agent is a distinct wireframe shape. A working agent shows it by its status ring, its chip and a slow pulse, and a tunnel runs in one solid line from the agent itself to the row of the file it touches, yellow while it works, with packets running along that line from the agent to the file, with four yellow corner brackets around that card. The tunnel goes from the agent to the root card and then down the same lit folder links the yellow string runs through, so the line and its packets sit on the yellow chain rather than on a straight line to the card; when the folder holding the file has no lit link to the root, it is the straight line from the agent to the file.
- Each agent has a terminal chip beside it, joined by a thin line. The main orchestrator and each sub put their chip on the side away from the hub centre on screen, chosen each frame from the projected x difference with a small dead band so it does not flip when the two are almost in line, while a lane keeps its own side. With a sub orchestrator in the room, the main's chip and callout rise 240 units (`ORCHESTRATOR_NAME_LIFT`, through the pure `orchestratorNameLift` in `agents.js`), so the main's name stays above a sub's shape from the overview at any heading. The chip shows the name and a status word, and opens to show the log. An agent whose latest tool call is `shell` or `other` also shows a cyan `CONSOLE` above its head with the last five log lines; it holds for 6 seconds after the call ends, fades with the agent, and a click selects the agent; when it finds an address, `addressUrl` makes it a full `http:` or `https:` URL and the address shows as an `a.mr-term-console-link` in its label that opens in a new tab without selecting the agent, while a click elsewhere on the console still selects it (`terminals.js`, U68 and U83).
- The selected agent shows in the dock's middle column: harness and model, status, task, current command, this month's cost and tokens, its last 12 log lines, and for an orchestrator its context share and the autocompact input: an on switch, a value in percent or tokens, the `Compacts at N of WINDOW tokens` line, and the compact rules with the default rules as the placeholder. A sub orchestrator also shows its lanes, scope, restriction and setter, and a lane shows its sector. The buttons are Focus, Log, Open ADDRESS when the console has an address, and Open chat. With nothing selected it shows the first working agent, or the orchestrator when none is working.
- The project folders are flat folder cards in one ring around the hub, all in the hub's own level plane and joined by straight lines: each top-level folder to the root card, which sits directly beneath the hub circle at the ring's centre with its top edge 240 units below the ring's level, so the ring's front edge stays above the card's head at the overview pitch, and every other folder to its parent; the root card has no line. The first top-level folder is at 12 o'clock, straight back from the camera, and the rest run clockwise at equal angles, so up to 12 folders land on the 12 hours, on a radius that grows with their count and never comes closer than 900 units to the hub centre, so the top-level cards do not overlap. More than 12 top-level folders leave the base ring in a square-like shotgun spray behind 12 o'clock, row after row further out, each row 380 units behind the one before. Each card lists its own files as rows. With the folder depth at N, every folder down to level N is its own card with its files, and its child folders branch outward from it in a square-like shotgun grid, a row of columns across its radial line, the first row 450 units further out and each later row 380 units further out, so four child folders sit in a 2 by 2 grid; nothing is drawn as a trail of stacked cards, and a folder whose children are all cards draws no backs. A folder with unexpanded child folders draws up to three folder blocks behind its face, each the face's size with a tab on its top edge, stepped 12 pixels left (`backOffsets` in `cards.js`), and its meta line counts the hidden folders beside the items, as in `3 items  2 folders`; the root card draws none. A click on a folder card pops it up 140 units out of the plane and draws its child folders as cards around it; a second click folds them back. An agent's path, a pin and a recent touch open a folder as before. Each string is a faint grey line at rest and brightens only while a working agent's path runs through it; a pin, a recent touch or a hover does not brighten it. The plan holds up to 300 folders from the depth setting, the root included, and a clicked folder adds its own children; a folder's children stay in its rows. The room plans every folder and file item a card holds and shows up to `maxFiles` rows (6 by default, within 4 to 20); a longer list scrolls inside the card, and a wheel over that list scrolls it instead of zooming the camera.
- Colour has one meaning each: white is activity, blue is the web, red is failure, yellow is active, green is finished work.
  - A read or write flashes the file row and its card white.
  - The folder and the file an agent works on right now carry a dashed yellow outline, and the strip from the agent to the file carries yellow stripes.
  - A failed tool call, including a failed test, flashes red and leaves the file red until it is written again. A failed run turns the agent and its chip red.
  - A run that ends well turns the agent, its chip and every file it wrote in that run green.
- There is no randomness. The same recording always draws the same room.
- The left sidebar (`Sidebar.jsx`) opens on three square tabs at its top (`SidebarTabs.jsx`), a line icon over a label each: Chat, Projects and Agents. The open tab carries a thin `#e5ff00` outline and the choice is saved as `sidebarTab` in `machineroom.settings`, `chat` by default. Chat holds `+ New conversation` as a rounded outlined button and the open project's conversations with pin, star, rename and delete, and the read-only preview with Continue this conversation. Projects holds the current project and the recent projects that open the room on their folder. Agents holds the family tree, open by default, titled `Agents` with the active lane count: the nodes from `family()`, the orchestrator at the root and its children indented 16 pixels a level with thin connectors, each row with its label, `harness · model · effort`, a state dot and, for the main and a sub, its state word (`working`, `waiting`, `paused`) and a chat button that dispatches `machineroom:open-chat` with `detail: { id }`, and, for a lane, the short task it runs while working, the active switch and a trash button; a turned-off lane stays in the tree with an off dot, a click on the switch turns a lane on or off at once, and a click on the trash button or Remove opens a danger confirm card; a click on the row opens the node's editor with the label, and for an orchestrator or a sub the harness, its models and effort list and the autocompact input; the Direct API harness shows its endpoint models as `NAME/MODEL` labelled `MODEL (NAME)`, has no key slot select and no Add key form, and with no endpoint says `Add a Direct API key in Settings first.` beside an `Open Settings` button that dispatches `machineroom:open-settings` to open Settings at this section; a lane's editor has its model and effort and Remove; the tree names the `machineroom` harness `Direct API` everywhere; every node has Add child, a confirm card covers every other write, and a Requests group lists the proposals with Approve and Decline; it refreshes on the `family` and `agents` notices and reads each method with an optional call, so a bridge without them leaves the section out. It is written for the lower half of the page and mounts at 900 pixels and wider when that frame is wired; below 900 pixels a Sessions entry in the folded menu opens it as a full width overlay with the same tabs. Without the bridge methods, such as a replay, it names the project and says `Projects and conversations appear when the room runs live.`

## Layout of the code

```
src/model/        pure logic, no DOM, fully tested
  paths.js          path cleanup, ancestors, tree builder
  commands.js       files and folders a shell command touches
  folderView.js     planFolderView: the cards to draw from the files, the focus, recent touches, pins and alwaysDepth, and `limitItems` false keeps every folder and file item a card holds
  liveTasks.js      agentTasks: the task label per agent for the callout and the family tree row
```

## Data flow

1. A source sends frames: the lane source from a `run-lane` log, or the sessions source from a session file. A frame is `{id, companyId, type, createdAt, payload}`.
2. `pipeline.js` turns frames into activities. `heartbeat.run.log` carries raw agent output in `payload.chunk`; the parser for that agent turns it into tool calls.
3. `state.js` applies each activity to a mutable room state. `player.js` owns the clock and calls `onActivity` for each applied activity and `onReset` after a seek.
4. `room.js` reads the state every frame and reacts to activities with short animations (pulses, flashes, folder opening). Animations run on wall time. State changes run on replay time.
5. `src/model/sessionRecorder.js` keeps the frames a page receives as a rolling log: nothing until a frame starts a recording, then every frame back at most a set number of minutes (10 by default, 1 to 240). A frame starts it when its type begins `heartbeat.run.` or its payload names a `runId` or an `agentId`; the session readers open a run for each conversation, so a conversation's first frame starts the log. `recorderToReplay` builds the object `prepare` builds, with `createdAt` and `payload.ts` from 0 at the oldest kept frame, and answers null when nothing was kept. `src/App.jsx` keeps one recorder for the page, `src/Stage.jsx` pushes every frame the live source receives into it, and a plain `?replay` with no `recording`, `fixture`, `tour` or `scale` plays `recordedReplay` of it with the agents and tree of the live room (V34). Before the first conversation the room says `Nothing recorded yet. Replay starts with the first conversation.`, and `recordMinutes` sets the window.

## Live mode

The first visit opens a connect screen (`src/ui/Connect.jsx`). The choice is saved in localStorage (`machineroom.settings`) with the replay speed, and the next visit reconnects by itself. A failed live connection returns to the connect screen with a message naming the address. While a live room has had no run yet, or the link is down, a waiting overlay (`Waiting.jsx`) says why; at 900 pixels and wider it sits inside the lens at its bottom middle, and below that it stays fixed over the page. A close button at the right end of its header hides the card until the feed status changes, so a new connection error or a state transition shows it again. Addresses override the saved choice: `?connect` shows the screen, `?replay` opens the replay of this page's recorded session, or of the fixture or recording the address names. Ways to connect:

On the first visit to a live room whose root is not a git checkout, and whenever a page dispatches the `machineroom:projects` window event, the project picker (`src/ui/Launcher.jsx`, flag `machineroom.launcher`) covers the lower half of the page: the question `Which folder do you want to work in?`, the current folder first as `Keep working in NAME` with Enter, then the recent projects and the folders beside the current one from `projects()` with a small `git` tag on a checkout, each row with its name and path, and a text box for any absolute folder path. Picking the current folder closes the card. Picking another calls `client.openProject({ path, confirmed: true })`, shows `Opening NAME...`, polls `GET /api/health` every 500 ms until it answers and `projects().current.path` is the new path (at most 30 seconds, then `The room did not come back. Run machineroom in that folder.`), and reloads the page; a 501 answer shows its sentence. Escape and Close set the flag, and `document.body` carries `has-launcher` while the card shows so the setup card waits. The ordering, the path check and the poll are pure in `src/ui/launcher.js`.

- `/?live&bridge=http://127.0.0.1:5174` goes through the bridge. The bridge lists the agents (one per lane or session reader), sends the project file list and pushes `tree` events when files appear (a recursive watcher while the project is quiet; on the first change it closes the watcher and polls, starting a read no sooner than three times the last read's length after it, until no change for 2 seconds; a folder-shaped `GET /api/tree` may reuse a read from the last second) to each page that opened `/events` with a `tree` parameter (the control client's stream asks for none and gets none): a page's first one holds only the files added since the `version` of the list it read with `GET /api/tree` (the page sends it as `since`; the bridge keeps its last 4 versions), or the whole list when it names no version the bridge still has, and each later one only the files that page has not had, since the page only adds files. A page that opens `/events?tree=folders`, or asks `GET /api/tree` with `Accept: application/json; shape=folders` (a CORS-safe value, so no preflight), gets the whole list in the folder shape `{ root, shape: 'folders', folders: [[parent, name]], files: [[folder, name, ...]] }`, which names each folder once; the folder answer of `GET /api/tree` also carries `version`, and it is gzipped (async `zlib.gzip`) when the request's `Accept-Encoding` names gzip. `readFileList` in `src/live/tree.js` reads both shapes, so pages and bridges of different versions still agree. The bridge lists a git project with `git ls-files -z`, so names with non-ASCII letters arrive as they are. `GET /api/health` answers `{ ok, lanes, sessions, orchestrator?, unparsed, masked }` (`lanes` and `sessions` are true when that source is on, and `orchestrator` is true when the lane source mounts the chat). `GET /api/mcp/servers`, `POST /api/mcp/servers`, `POST /api/mcp/servers/NAME/delete`, `GET /api/mcp/tools` and `POST /api/mcp/call` manage the MCP servers in `<runs>/mcp.json` and call their tools (H14). `unparsed` is `{ total, byParser, since }`: the stdout lines shaped like a tool call that no parser could read since the bridge started, counted in `accept()` with `createUnreadableCounter` so lines split over two chunks count once. `masked` is `{ total, since }`: `accept()` passes each frame through `createSplitMasker` (`src/briefs/audit.js`), which runs `maskKeys` on a frame's `chunk`, `message` and `error` and masks a key cut across two log chunks of one run and stream, before the frame goes into the history or to a page, so a key an agent prints reaches the page as `[key]`; the bridge never logs what it masked. `POST /api/decide` asks the decision model (`server/decide.js` over `decide()` in `src/decider`), with the key from `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and the model from the body, `DECISION_MODEL` or `typesafe/jev-1.14`; without a key it answers 404, and it logs only the model, the question names, the result and the time. `GET /api/projects` answers `{ current: { path, name, git }, recent: [{ path, name, openedAt, sessions }], folders: [{ path, name, git }] }`: `recent` leaves out the current root and folders that no longer exist, missing folders leave `<runs>/projects.json` when it is read (at most once a minute), `sessions` holds at most 6 `{ id, title, endedAt }` from `<runs>/orchestrator-<hash>.json`, and `folders` lists sibling subfolders of the parent (at most 200, by name, with `.git`). `recordProject` skips folders inside the OS temp folder (`os.tmpdir()` and `TEMP`), lane worktrees (with a `.worktrees` folder or in `docs/agents/lanes.json`), and runs folders (`runs.jsonl`). `POST /api/projects/open` takes `{ path, confirmed: true }`, requires a page Origin, refuses a path that is not absolute, missing or not a folder with 400 and one sentence, answers 409 while an orchestrator turn runs, adds the path to `<runs>/projects.json` (at most 20, newest first, the current root added when the bridge starts), answers `{ ok: true, restarting: true, path }`, and calls `onOpenProject(path)` to restart the bridge on the new root, or answers 501 `Open another project with machineroom FOLDER.` when started without the option. `POST /api/projects/forget` takes `{ path, confirmed: true }`, requires a page Origin (403 without), refuses a path not in the list with 400, removes that entry from `projects.json`, and answers the new list (`{ current, recent, folders }`).
- `--lanes` (or `MACHINEROOM_LANES=1`) makes the bridge show lane runs live: it watches the runs folder (`MACHINEROOM_RUNS`, else `%TEMP%\machineroom-runs`) for a new `<lane>-<brief>-<stamp>.log`, reads it from its first byte as it grows, and sends `heartbeat.run.queued`, `heartbeat.run.status` running, one `heartbeat.run.log` per complete line the harness parser reads (a line with no activity sends no frame) with `stream: 'stdout'` and a growing `seq`, then `heartbeat.run.status` succeeded or failed. The held partial line is sent before the end frame; past 1 MB it is dropped and the reader skips to the next newline. Its frames go through the same path as the sessions' frames, so they are masked, kept in the history and sent on `/events`; the bridge keeps an open run's queued and running frames out of the 500-frame eviction, so a late page always gets a run's start. It ends a run on its record in `runs.jsonl` (matched on `log`, using `exitCode` and `killed`; the file is read again only when its size or mtime changed, and only from where the last read stopped) or after 10 minutes with no growth and no record (`failed`, `error: 'no record'`), but not while the lane's `<lane>.lock` names a live run or harness process. A log that grows after a quiet end reopens the run with a new `running` frame and the same `runId`. ## Room behaviour

- Space: black, with a faint lattice in all three axes (2000 unit cells, brighter tick crosses, a few coordinate labels), a far shell of dust and fog. Every far speck and every fine dust mote drifts at its own speed, up to about 36 units a second for a far speck and 18 for a mote, with its own wander rate and phase on each axis, and soft-bounces at the inner and outer radius of its shell, so the cloud keeps its shape and count; no dust layer turns as a group, the positions are written every second frame, only for the specks the strength draws, with nothing allocated per frame, and each mote's own colour sets its pulse. The camera drifts a little with the pointer, eased, and not at all under reduced motion, and the dust holds its specks where they are.
- Hub: a flat ring at the middle, radius 300. Each top-level agent other than the orchestrator has a slot on it in roster order and stays parked there, working or not; the orchestrator floats above the hub centre. With a ladder of one or more subs, `sectorArcs` splits the ring into one arc per sector, with a gap at each end and the sector's name outside the ring, just inside the start of the arc and below its plane, and each lane parks inside its sector's arc in roster order. A sub orchestrator floats above the middle of its arc at 0.7 of the main orchestrator's size, on the orchestrator's armillary shape, joined to it by a thin white line. With no ladder or an empty one the ring and slots are as before. ## Activity schema

Every activity has `time` (ms since epoch), `agent` (key), `runId` and `order`.

| type | extra fields |
| --- | --- |
| `run.queued` | none |
| `run.start` | none |
| `run.end` | `ok`, `error` |
| `tool.start` | `id`, `tool` (read, write, edit, shell, search, web, other), `label`, `touches` |
| `tool.end` | `id`, `ok` |
| `error` | `text` |
| `agent.message` | `to` (the receiving agent key) and `kind` (`order`, `report`, `dispatch` or `wake`); `agent` is the sender |
| `agent.spawn` | `child` (the parent key, `~` and a number from 1 within the parent's run), `name` (at most 40 characters), `label`; `agent` is the parent |
| `agent.end` | `ok`; `agent` is the child |

The OpenCode and Hermes parsers emit `agent.spawn` and then `agent.end` at the time of a `subagent` or `delegate` line, since those harnesses report a child only when it has finished, and keep the `other` tool call for the same line. The Claude parser reads Claude Code's own stream-json (S6g): `Read`, `Write`, `Edit`, `MultiEdit` and `NotebookEdit` touch `file_path` or `notebook_path`, `Glob` and `Grep` touch a folder, `LS` lists one, and `Bash` reads its command. A `Task` or `Agent` tool call starts the child as `PARENT~N`, every later line whose `parent_tool_use_id` is that call's id carries the child's key so the child has its own tool calls and strip, and that call's `tool_result` ends it, failed when `is_error` is set. The AGY parser shows a child while it works, from what a real AGY run sends (S5, run `ffd660e6` on 2026-10-24): an `invoke_subagent` tool step (`Role` is the child's name) starts it as `PARENT~N`, labelled with the `initial_prompt` its `DONE` step carries in `subagent_info`, and it ends at the first `manage_subagents` list whose output shows it in a state other than `running`, or with the parent's `run.end`. The child's own steps go to a transcript file, not to the parent's output, so the room shows the child working with its role and prompt but no strip of its own. This AGY's file tools are `read_file`, `write_file` and `edit_file` (with `Path`), beside the older names. `scenario-g` is that real run, masked; S4's rule (a second `conversation_id` in one run is a child) stays, tested by the hand-written `scenario-f`, though the real AGY never sent one. `state.js` adds a child to the roster with `parent` and `depth` (at most 3), and the parent's `run.end` ends its children still working. The engine adds a view for a child when it appears (S2d): the next spare shape at 0.6 of its parent's size beside the parked parent (`childPlace`), a short line to it, and a chip titled `PARENT > NAME`; a child that has ended stays at full opacity for 6 seconds of wall time and then fades over 1.5 seconds (`childFade`, pure, tested; under reduced motion it goes at once after the 6 seconds). The HUD lists a child under its parent, indented 16px a level, with a `subagent` tag (`.hud-agent-row.is-subagent`, `.agent-subagent`); the number keys pick top agents only.

A touch is `{path, mode, kind}`. `kind` is `file` or `dir`. `mode` is `read`, `write` or `list`. `path` is relative to the project root, and `''` is the root.

## Conventions

- Plain words, short sentences. No em dashes. No single-line explanatory comments.
- Fonts: Jost and Space Mono, weights 400 and 500 only. Never bold.
- Colours: white, `#e5ff00` (active), `#ff2a2a` (failure), `#2aff80` (finished), `#2a9dff` (web) on black. Spacing on an 8px base.
- Commit message: version prefix, then a sentence. Example: `0.1.1: Add the live bridge`.
- Keep large binaries and secrets out of git. Never print a key.

## Known limits

- Only the top-level ring is spaced from the card sizes (each card's diagonal plus a 40 unit gap), so top-level cards never overlap. A folder's children are placed in one pass: their fan is a fixed offset per sibling, so siblings in a deep fan can overlap by design (V27).
- The overview fits the hub, the root and the top-level ring from the overview angle, aimed at the hub centre (V18, V27). In the seeded replay its distance is a median of 1,754 units (largest 1,754) at 40,000 files and 1,826 (1,826) at 200,000, down from 3,633 (4,129) and 3,264 (3,647) after U24, because the room is flat and the fit no longer reaches up a sphere (`npm run scale-probe` prints the `overview distance` row). The projected ring is wider than it is tall, so the fit fills the lens width and leaves the top and bottom emptier. Each name stays at 11 pixels or more on screen. The covered row is 0 of 399 layouts at 40,000 files on 2026-10-26, and following the touched card (V15) is what makes the rows readable.
- Hermes read lines carry only a file name. When two files share it, the parser picks the one in or nearest the folder of that run's last file; with no folder yet, or a tie, the name lands at the root.
- During a tool call that names no file (most shell commands), an agent keeps its last file as its focus, so the strip and the follow camera stay on it; the owner chose this over clearing it (audit finding V3).
- Failure is read from tool status and from output text such as a test marker. A tool that fails quietly shows as ok.
- In live mode without a file list, a Hermes read shows only a file name, so a file it reads before any other agent has touched it appears at the root until its real path is seen.
- `npm run serve` serves the built viewer and the bridge on one port, so a production build needs no dev server.
- The bridge and the scripts start git by its absolute path from PATH (`gitPath()` in `server/programPath.js`), because Node on Windows runs a `git.exe` from the child's working folder first when `NoDefaultCurrentDirectoryInExePath` is not set, and a lane worktree is such a folder. They name `refs/heads/main`, never `main`, so a tag cannot stand in for the branch. A run and its children stop through `killTree()` in `server/processTree.js` (`taskkill /T /F` on Windows, the process group elsewhere). On Windows it leaves a child object that has already exited alone, because its process id may belong to another process by then; `takeRoomLock`, `takeLaneLock` and `stopLane` treat a lock as stale when its process id was created after the lock file's last change plus 2 seconds, `stopLane` passes its injected list and kill down to `stopDescendants` and `stopLeftovers`, and `descendantProcesses` and `leftoverProcesses` ignore a parent created after its child (H19, Q16b). When the harness itself has exited, or after its final `result` line, `startLane` waits 30 seconds for `close`, then stops what is left with the descendant and leftover helpers, destroys the pipes and finishes the run with the harness's own exit code, `killed: false` and a note on the record that leftovers were stopped (H20). On Linux a child that left the harness's process group and outlives it has init as its parent, so the run still ends and its pipes close, but that child keeps running unless its command line names the lane's worktree, and the note reads the same.
- The bridge allows a page only from `http://localhost:PORT` or `http://127.0.0.1:PORT` on its own port, from the Vite port 5173 only when the bridge has no `staticDir` (the dev server case), and from each exact origin in `MACHINEROOM_ORIGINS` (a comma list of `http://localhost:N` or `http://127.0.0.1:N`; other entries are skipped with a printed line). Any other Origin gets 403. A request with no Origin passes the origin gate, but `POST /api/control/reviews/LANE/merge` needs the page's Origin or a valid orchestrator token, every `/api/mcp` route and `GET /api/harnesses/KEY/models` need the page's Origin or a same-origin `Sec-Fetch-Site` header, and `POST /api/assistant` and `POST /api/decide` need the page's Origin; the assistant, decide and MCP call routes also need `confirmed: true` (U71). A lane run needs `confirmed: true`, and a merge also needs the lane's exact head sha.
- The bridge masks a key that is split across chunks of one run and stream (`createSplitMasker` in `src/briefs/audit.js`): it holds a chunk whose last line could still grow into a key, and every later frame, for at most 1 second. A key whose head was released after that second, unmasked because it was not yet a whole key, reaches the page as that head and `[key]` in the next frame. The splitter starts a new line, so text from before a gap is never joined to the chunk after it, when a chunk's `seq` is not its run's last `seq` plus one, when a chunk still has `truncated: true`, and when the socket opens again (`reset()`, Q1c).
- About half of the full test runs on this machine die inside `node.exe` 24.15.0 with 0xC0000409 (bash shows exit 127) while the test files that start a bridge run (`bridge`, `control-contract`, `bridge-reviews`). The Windows event log names Node itself at a fixed address, and a probe hit the libuv assertion `!(handle->flags & UV_HANDLE_CLOSING)` in `src\win\async.c` when a process exited right after `bridge.stop()`. The forks pool still crashes, and one worker avoids it but is 3.6 times slower (T15). A crashed run leaves its `mr-*` temp folders behind. `npm test` runs them again by itself (T15b); it is not a test failure. After the owner moved to Node 24.22.1 (2026-10-22), three full parallel runs in a row passed with no retry; the retry stays until more runs agree.
- On this machine a test that starts git or a bridge takes 1 to 4 seconds alone and up to 11 while a lane runs its own suite, so `vite.config.js` allows 20 seconds a test and a hook instead of the default 5.
- The full test run is bounded by `tests/bridge-reviews.test.js`, which took 96 seconds alone on 2026-10-25 while other lanes ran their own suites. A paired full run in one quiet window took 90.5 seconds before T1 and 51.3 after it; pairs under other lanes' load took 150 and 201 seconds before and 103 and 176 after, so the wall time follows that file and the machine's load. T1 removed the repeated git and bridge setup and left all 2151 tests running. Three full runs under other lanes' load took 104, 42 and 59 seconds before, with one lane lock failure, and 63, 78 and 42 seconds after, with 2360 tests and no failure. In the loaded runs after, the slowest test took 16.3 seconds, the review scan of the real checkout took 15.5, the project fast forward fell from 22.6 to 12.2 at its worst, and the worktree reuse test fell from 6.6 to 3.0.
- T26 ran `npm test` eight times in the opencode3 worktree on 2026-10-30 while other lanes ran their suites, before H19: six passed and two failed, on `tests/project-reviews.test.js` (a merge answered 404 where it should answer 409), `tests/orchestrators.test.js` (`branchChanges` returned null) and `tests/project-dispatch.test.js` (a 20 second time out); the cause is not known yet (T28). - On a project of 40000 files `GET /api/tree` holds about 0.6 MB in the folder shape and 115 KB gzipped, and on 200000 files about 3.1 MB and 570 KB gzipped (P3d, P13); a page's first `tree` event then holds only the files added since that answer.
