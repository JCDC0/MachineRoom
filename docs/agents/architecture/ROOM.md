# Room

What the room shows and how it behaves.

## What it shows

- The page has one menu row at the top, then the lens, the transport and the lower half stacked in one window-high frame at 900 pixels and wider, so the page does not scroll. Two rails sit on the lens (`LensRail.jsx`, `src/ui/lensResize.js`): Height on its left edge sets the lens from 30 to 85 percent of the window (`--lens-h-user`, saved as `lensSplit` in `machineroom.settings`), 56 percent by default, and every height is capped at `min(calc(100vh - var(--menu-h) - var(--transport-h)), max(240px, Nvh))` so the transport always stays on screen; Zoom on its right edge moves the camera on a log scale between its nearest and furthest distance, 500 and 8000 units (`zoomShare`, `setZoomShare`, `zoomBy` and `zoomMultiplier` on the camera rig and the room); each shows at 0.18 opacity with its label and multiplier hidden at rest, shows in full while the pointer is within 64 pixels of its own lens edge (`railNear`), while it is dragged, for a second after a wheel turn over it, or while its track has keyboard focus, over 0.2 seconds and at once under reduced motion, and follows a drag, the wheel over it and the keys. At 900 pixels and wider the transport sits between the lens and the lower half, and the lower half (`.hud-lower`, U34) takes whatever height is left under the menu, lens and transport; it works like the Antigravity app, in three columns that scroll on their own. The sidebar column is `clamp(280px, 22vw, 340px)` wide; the right column keeps its 280 pixels above 1100 pixels and narrows below that to `clamp(200px, calc(40vw - 160px), 280px)`, so the conversation takes what is left; a log line moves a path to its own line and breaks inside it only when the path is longer than the line, and its agent column fits `CLAUDE CODE`, so a path and a name stay whole; a family tree row gives its label the full first line, with the state word or task, the kind tag, `harness · model · effort`, the switch, the trash and `+` on the second line. In a live room at 900 pixels and wider the transport's middle shows `ORCHESTRATOR` with the main orchestrator's state word (`working`, `waiting for you`, `paused`), and a 22 pixel context ring with its percent sits just left of the clock: the arc fills with `contextTokens / contextWindow`, white below the autocompact rule, yellow at or past it and red past 95 percent, the ring's title is `N of WINDOW tokens`, and with no numbers it is an empty ring with `context not reported`; a click on either focuses the chat input (`src/ui/contextRing.js`, U42). With subs, a compact chip per sub follows the state word, up to four and then `+N`: the sub's name and a state dot, yellow while it works, dim when idle and red when paused or failed, from the orchestrators list Stage reads (`src/ui/transportOrchestrators.js`, U80). A click on a chip focuses the chat input and opens that sub's chat tab through the `machineroom:open-chat` window event with `detail: { id }`. After the sub chips, one compact pill per lane that is queued, working or failed in its last run: its label, the brief or task id from `liveRuns()` and `issues()` as `agentTasks` reads them, a state dot (yellow working, red failed), the minutes since the run started and `N/M` when the run is a queue whose output says its step, from the runs answer; the lanes at work come first and the failed ones newest first, at most six and then `+N`, and a failed pill leaves when its lane runs again. A click dispatches `machineroom:inspect` for that lane, which focuses and inspects it (`src/ui/transportLanes.js`, U120). A failed pill reads that lane's newest record from `last`, not only the newest 20 in `recent` (U129). Just left of the context ring the transport shows `$X.XX today · $Y.YY mo` in Space Mono, the sum over the lanes of today's and this month's spend from the cost board; a click scrolls the cost board into view in the right column, and with no cost reported it shows nothing (`src/ui/transportSpend.js`, U120). The left column is the sidebar (`Sidebar.jsx`, U35 and U45), three tabs at its top (`SidebarTabs.jsx`) with a line icon and a label each: Chat, Projects and Agents, the open one inside a thin `#e5ff00` outline, saved as `sidebarTab` in `machineroom.settings` (`chat` by default). Chat holds a rounded outlined `+ New conversation` and the open project's conversations (pin, star, rename, delete, a read-only preview with Continue); Projects holds the current project and the recent projects that open the room on their folder; Agents holds `Build a team` (`FamilyTeam.jsx`, U81) above the family tree (`FamilyTree.jsx`, U43): it takes 1 to 4 orchestrators under the main and 1 to 3 coders each plus a harness and model for the new subs, and previews `Team 1`, `Team 2` and their lanes from the active lanes in picker order and then the idle ones, never a lane already in a sub; too few lanes says `Turn on N more lanes first.` and hides Save, and Save opens a confirm card that lists every sub and its lanes, then saves the ladder as the owner with the existing subs kept and sets each new sub's label. The family tree has the orchestrator at the root and its children indented 16 pixels a level with thin connectors, each row with its label, `harness · model · effort`, a state dot and, for a lane, an active switch and a selection checkbox; one or more checked lanes show a compact bar above the rows with `N selected`, Add sub orchestrator and Clear, and that form takes a label, harness, model and effort and saves a sub orchestrator over the checked lanes through the ladder with no new lane (U54); a click opens the node's editor, an orchestrator or a sub has Add child and a lane's `+` opens the sub form with that lane checked, a sub row and its editor carry Remove behind `Remove "LABEL"? Its agents return to the orchestrator.`, and a sub's editor lists every code agent in an Agents checklist saved through the ladder (U54); a Requests group at the top lists the agents an orchestrator proposed with Approve and Decline. The middle is the conversation: a breadcrumb `PROJECT / CONVERSATION`, the orchestrator's messages in a centred column, and the input box with the model and effort pickers, Compact with its autocompact value and rules, the context size and Stop; a replay shows the event log there instead. The right column shows the inspector when something is inspected, in place of the Agent panel; its Close and Escape return it to the rest view of `AgentDock` for the selected agent, else the first working agent, else the orchestrator, above the cost board, with both always open and no Overview card, drawer or back control; Log and Settings open from the menu as dropdowns under it, like Control. A first live visit asks which folder to work in (`Launcher.jsx`, U36, which opens another project through the bridge, O12), then how many agents should work (`SetupCard.jsx`, U33), then runs the outlined tour. Below 900 pixels the dock stays open, the orchestrator chat floats at the bottom right, and Sessions in the folded menu opens the sidebar as an overlay.
- In a live room the CONTROL action carries a small yellow count of the lane branches with commits ahead, read once at the start, every 5 minutes, when a run ends and when Control closes, and a click on that count opens Control on Reviews; the count is its own button, so the keyboard reaches it (`Hud.jsx`, U129); the ORCHESTRATOR action carries one for pending proposals plus paused orchestrators, since an idle one is only resting. A count of 0 shows nothing.
- A live room shows a red failure tray at the lens's bottom right, above the newest log line: this page session's failed runs and failed tool calls, newest first, five at a time with +N, each row with the agent, the failed tool or the exit code and the age; a click inspects that agent, Clear empties it, a lane's rows leave when it ends a run well, and a replay shows none.
- The middle is the hub: a flat ring where each top-level agent other than the orchestrator stays at its own slot, working or not; the orchestrator floats above the hub centre. When run-lane records the Claude Code or OpenCode session that started it, the room puts that session in the orchestrator's place at the top, with lane-start stars leaving from it. Each lane is joined by a faint grey line to the orchestrator that leads it, its sub when the ladder puts it in one and the main otherwise, and a sub orchestrator keeps its own thin white line to the main; both are drawn under the agents and do not change while an agent works. With a ladder of sub orchestrators, the ring splits into one named arc per sector, and a sub orchestrator floats above its arc at 0.7 of the main orchestrator's size. A sector with one lane parks it at the middle of its arc, under its sub; two or more lanes sit evenly inside the arc with the same gap at each end. The sector's name outside the ring, just inside the start of its arc and a little below the ring plane, is the sub's name in capitals. `setWorking` names the agents at work: a sector with one of them brightens its arc to full white and returns it over 1 second when none works (at once under reduced motion), and the rest of the ring keeps its brightness. The room passes it the keys of the agents working in the room state, a sub orchestrator and its lanes included, and calls it only when that set changes.
- Each agent is a distinct wireframe shape and has its own colour, from the seven `AGENT_COLORS` in `sprites.js` by roster order (`agentColor`; a child takes its parent's). A working agent shows it by its status ring, its chip and a slow pulse, and a tunnel runs as a grey light trail (`TUNNEL_GREY`, `#9a9a9a`) from the agent's centre to the tab of the card that holds the file it touches: a core about 2 pixels wide and a sheath about 9 pixels wide at a quarter of the core's opacity, at 0.45 opacity, grey while it works, green for 5 seconds after a good run and red on a failure. The trail eases in over 0.3 seconds when the agent starts on a file and out over 0.6 seconds, and a card still hides the part behind it as it hides the other lines. It goes to the root card and then down the lit folder links, with every waypoint on that folder's tab anchor (`cards.anchor`); when the touched folder's chain does not pass through the root card's link the root waypoint is left out, and when the folder has no lit link to the root it is the straight trail. The card it reaches shows four corner brackets in the agent's colour, and the touched file's name shows small camera brackets in the same colour (`--mr-target`): the brackets mean that agent is touching it. Each touch sends one star in the agent's colour along that route, and the row flash and the `read NAME` label wait for it to land (Stars).
- Each agent has a terminal chip beside it, joined by a thin line. The main orchestrator and each sub put their chip on the side away from the hub centre on screen, chosen each frame from the projected x difference with a small dead band so it does not flip when the two are almost in line, while a lane keeps its own side. With a sub orchestrator in the room, the main's chip and callout rise 240 units (`ORCHESTRATOR_NAME_LIFT`, through the pure `orchestratorNameLift` in `agents.js`), so the main's name stays above a sub's shape from the overview at any heading. A closed chip is a 98 pixel box with its state: the state dot and the state word. The name moves to its aria label and stays in the label under the agent. An open chip keeps its title and its log. A finished chip is black with a 1 pixel `#2aff80` border and `#2aff80` text, with no fill or glow, so it does not outshine a working chip. Above four agents at work, only the focused agent, the agent under the pointer and the two that started work most recently show the full callout with its task and command; the other agents show the name block alone (`fullCalloutKeys`, with `CALLOUT_CROWD_LIMIT` 4 and `CALLOUT_RECENT_KEEP` 2, pure, tested). An agent whose latest tool call is `shell` or `other` also shows a cyan `CONSOLE` above its head with the last five log lines; it holds for 6 seconds after the call ends, fades with the agent, and a click selects the agent; when it finds an address, `addressUrl` makes it a full `http:` or `https:` URL and the address shows as an `a.mr-term-console-link` in its label that opens in a new tab without selecting the agent, while a click elsewhere on the console still selects it (`terminals.js`, U68 and U83).
- The selected agent shows in the dock's middle column: harness and model, status, task, current command, this month's cost and tokens, its last 12 log lines, and for an orchestrator its context share and the autocompact input: an on switch, a value in percent or tokens, the `Compacts at N of WINDOW tokens` line, and the compact rules with the default rules as the placeholder. A sub orchestrator also shows its lanes, scope, restriction and setter, and a lane shows its sector. The buttons are Focus, Log, Open ADDRESS when the console has an address, and Open chat. With nothing selected it shows the first working agent, or the orchestrator when none is working.
- The inspector opens in the right column of the lower half at 900 pixels and wider, and as a fixed panel over the dock below that, from a click on an agent, a folder, a card row, a file, the INTERNET sphere or the git tree, and from the `machineroom:inspect` window event with `{ kind, id }` (`Inspector.jsx`, helper `inspector.js`, U94). An agent shows its label, harness, model and effort, its state word (`thinking` while a run is open and no tool call is open, `working` with the tool and command while one is, else `idle`, `failed` or `done`), its task, its context share and tokens when it is an orchestrator and `context not reported` for a lane, this month's cost and tokens in a live room, its last 12 log lines and, for an orchestrator, the last messages of its chat with Open chat. A folder lists a breadcrumb and its folders then files from the room's whole file tree, at most 200 rows, and `N more. Open the folder card to see them all.` when it is cut; an inner folder or a breadcrumb calls `focusPath` and the panel follows it. A file shows its path, size, last change and every agent touch with read or write and the time, then a plain text preview from `client.fileInfo` with `Too large, showing the first 256 KB.`, `Binary file.` or the bridge's sentence. The INTERNET sphere shows `Connected` or `No connection` and the agents on the web now, and a replay leaves out the preview and the cost. The git tree shows `Source control`: a Branch row or `Detached at SHA`, `Changes N` with a row per uncommitted path cut from the left so the file name shows, and its status letter on the right, where a click selects that file, a path ending in / selects that folder, and a deleted file is disabled, or `No uncommitted changes.`, then `Graph`, one 24 pixel row per commit with an inline SVG at 12 pixels a lane, the head dot `#e5ff00` and the rest white, the subject cut with an ellipsis, a thin outlined badge per ref with the current branch in yellow, and `Showing the newest 200 commits.` when the list is cut. It reads `client.gitState()` on open, every 10 seconds and on a `git` notice, and shows `This folder is not a git repository.` for `{ git: false }` and nothing without the method or in a replay. Long lists scroll inside the panel.
- The project folders are flat folder cards in one ring around the hub, all in the hub's own level plane and joined by straight lines between their tabs: each top-level folder to the root card, which sits directly beneath the hub circle at the ring's centre with its top edge 240 units below the ring's level, so the ring's front edge stays above the card's head at the overview pitch, and every other folder to its parent; the root card has no parent line. The first top-level folder is at 12 o'clock, straight back from the camera, and the rest run clockwise at equal angles, so up to 12 folders land on the 12 hours, on a radius that grows with their count and never comes closer than 900 units to the hub centre, so the top-level cards do not overlap. More than 12 top-level folders leave the base ring in a square-like shotgun spray behind 12 o'clock, row after row further out, each row 380 units behind the one before. Each card lists its own files as rows. With the folder depth at N, every folder down to level N is its own card with its files, and its child folders branch outward from it in a square-like shotgun grid, a row of columns across its radial line, the first row 450 units further out and each later row 380 units further out, so four child folders sit in a 2 by 2 grid; nothing is drawn as a trail of stacked cards, and a folder whose children are all cards draws no backs. A folder with unexpanded child folders draws up to three folder blocks behind its face, each the face's size with a tab on its top edge, stepped 12 pixels left (`backOffsets` in `cards.js`), and its meta line counts the hidden folders beside the items, as in `3 items  2 folders`; the root card draws none. A folder named `node_modules`, `dist`, `build`, `coverage`, `vendor`, `.next`, `target`, `out` or `__pycache__` stays one closed card with no child cards until an agent touches a path inside it, a pin names it or the owner clicks it, and the root still draws every top-level folder (`isBulkFolder`, pure, tested). The child folders a card draws rank by focus and recent touches first, then by changed files when `changed` names them, then by file count, then by name. A click on a folder card pops it up 140 units out of the plane and draws its child folders as cards around it; a second click folds them back. An agent's path, a pin and a recent touch open a folder as before. Each string is a faint grey line at rest and brightens only while a working agent's path runs through it; a pin, a recent touch or a hover does not brighten it. The plan holds up to 300 folders from the depth setting, the root included. A folder below the root draws at most 10 child folders as cards, those with the most files, and the rest stay in its rows; the root draws every top-level folder. A clicked folder draws at most 10 of its children, ordered by focus and touch rank, then the most files, then name, and the root draws them all. A folder an agent touches or a pin still gets its card. The room plans every folder and file item a card holds, up to 2,000 for one folder with the touched ones first and a `+ N more` row for the rest, and shows up to `maxFiles` rows (6 by default, within 4 to 20); a longer list scrolls inside the card, and above 60 items only the rows in the scroll window plus four on each side are drawn, between two spacers that keep the scrollbar's height and position, redrawn on the list's `scroll` event, and a wheel over that list scrolls it instead of zooming the camera.
- Colour has one meaning each: white is activity, blue is the web, red is failure, yellow is active, green is finished work.
  - A read or write flashes the file row white when its star lands; the card does not flash.
  - The folder and the file an agent works on right now carry camera brackets in that agent's colour: four corner brackets on the card and small brackets around the file's name, with the name in that colour.
  - The selected folder carries a solid 2 pixel yellow border and a soft yellow glow, and its tab and name are yellow.
  - A failed tool call, including a failed test, flashes red and leaves the file red until it is written again. A failed run turns the agent and its chip red.
  - A run that ends well turns the agent, its chip and every file it wrote in that run green.
- There is no randomness. The same recording always draws the same room.
- The connect screen offers the tour as the sample run, the replay of what this page has seen since its first conversation with no fixed length, the demo and the bridge form. Its recovery line leads with `machineroom YOUR-PROJECT` and says to run `npm run serve -- up --root YOUR-PROJECT` in the MachineRoom folder.
- The left sidebar (`Sidebar.jsx`) opens on three square tabs at its top (`SidebarTabs.jsx`), a line icon over a label each: Chat, Projects and Agents. The open tab carries a thin `#e5ff00` outline and the choice is saved as `sidebarTab` in `machineroom.settings`, `chat` by default. Chat holds `+ New conversation` as a rounded outlined button, a search box, sort buttons Newest, Oldest and Name (kept as `machineroom.sessionSort`, Newest by default), and the open project's conversations grouped Pinned, Recent and Archived. Each row has one `...` menu with Pin or Unpin, Rename, Fork, Mark as unread, Archive or Unarchive, and Delete, with the keys P, R, F, U, A and D and a disabled item's reason as its title; the current conversation cannot be archived or deleted. While the menu is open every menu key is consumed, even when its item is disabled, and only an enabled item acts, so the room's shortcuts never see it. The menu closes when its row leaves the list or stops being current, and it holds the row's start time and title, so a conversation switch closes it and its keys never act on another conversation (P134, P142). An unread row shows a dot, and opening its preview clears the mark. Archived rows stay behind `Show archived (N)`. Fork reads the conversation's current message count from the bridge, opens a saved conversation first, and branches at that count into `TITLE (branch)`. The list says `Conversations are not available.` when the bridge refuses it, and it refreshes its counts after a chat turn ends. The preview is read only with Continue this conversation. Projects holds the current project and the recent projects that open the room on their folder. A row whose `git` is false, the open project's row too, carries one dim line under it, `Not a git repository. Run git init here.`, with a Copy button that copies `git init` and reads `Copied.` At the top of Projects, a live room shows the Repository section (`RepositoryPanel.jsx`, U92): the current branch or `Detached at SHA`, `Uncommitted changes` when the checkout is dirty, a list of the local branches that scrolls inside 192 pixels with the current one in `#e5ff00`, `ahead N`, `behind N` and `in a worktree`, a Switch button on every other branch behind the card `Switch PROJECT to BRANCH? New lanes start from it and reviews compare against it.` that is disabled while dirty or while a worktree holds the branch, and a New branch form with a name and a `from` select behind `Create NAME from FROM? The new branch is made without switching to it.`; `Show the tree` dispatches `machineroom:show-git-tree`, which opens the Source control panel and holds the camera on the tree. It reads `client.gitState()` on mount, every 60 seconds and on a `git` notice, keeps only the newest answer, and leaves the section out without that method, in a replay or when the answer says `{ git: false }`. Agents holds the family tree, open by default, titled `Agents` with the active lane count: the nodes from `family()`, the orchestrator at the root, any session that started lanes indented under it, and its children indented 16 pixels a level with thin connectors, each row with its label, `harness · model · effort`, a state dot and, for the main and a sub, its state word (`working`, `waiting`, `paused`) and a chat button that dispatches `machineroom:open-chat` with `detail: { id }`, and, for a lane, the short task it runs while working, the active switch and a trash button; a turned-off lane stays in the tree with an off dot, a click on the switch turns a lane on or off at once, and a click on the trash button or Remove opens a danger confirm card; a click on the row opens the node's editor with the label, and for an orchestrator or a sub the harness, its models and effort list and the autocompact input; the Direct API harness shows its endpoint models as `NAME/MODEL` labelled `MODEL (NAME)`, has no key slot select and no Add key form, and with no endpoint says `Add a Direct API key in Settings first.` beside an `Open Settings` button that dispatches `machineroom:open-settings` to open Settings at this section; a lane's editor has its model and effort, Save only on a lane the owner made, and Remove; the tree names the `machineroom` harness `Direct API` everywhere; every node has Add child, a confirm card covers every other write, and a Requests group lists the proposals with Approve and Decline; it refreshes on the `family` and `agents` notices and reads each method with an optional call, so a bridge without them leaves the section out. It is written for the lower half of the page and mounts at 900 pixels and wider when that frame is wired; below 900 pixels a Sessions entry in the folded menu opens it as a full width overlay with the same tabs. Without the bridge methods, such as a replay, it names the project and says `Projects and conversations appear when the room runs live.`

## Room behaviour

- Space: black, with a faint lattice of 2000 unit cells on two layers, one above the hub at `+LATTICE_STEP` and one below at `-LATTICE_STEP`, joined by vertical lines that stop at them, with brighter tick crosses on those two layers, a far shell of dust and fog. Every far speck and every fine dust mote drifts at its own speed, up to about 36 units a second for a far speck and 18 for a mote, with its own wander rate and phase on each axis, and soft-bounces at the inner and outer radius of its shell, so the cloud keeps its shape and count; no dust layer turns as a group, the positions are written every second frame, only for the specks the strength draws, with nothing allocated per frame, and each mote's own colour sets its pulse; a dust layer that is hidden or draws no speck is not walked and uploads nothing, and the colours are written only while Pulse is on and once when it turns off (P27). The camera drifts a little with the pointer, eased, and not at all under reduced motion, and the dust holds its specks where they are.
- Hub: a flat ring at the middle, radius 300. Each top-level agent other than the orchestrator has a slot on it in roster order and stays parked there, working or not; the orchestrator floats above the hub centre. With a ladder of one or more subs, `sectorArcs` splits the ring into one arc per sector, with a gap at each end and the sector's name outside the ring, just inside the start of the arc and below its plane, and each lane parks inside its sector's arc in roster order. With a ladder, a second, fainter line ring of 0.6 the hub's radius sits 260 units below the hub, and a sub's own lanes park on it inside their sector's arc at `subRingPlace`, while a lane in no sub stays on the main ring; a sub orchestrator floats above the middle of its arc at 0.7 of the main orchestrator's size, on the orchestrator's armillary shape, joined to it by a thin white line, so it sits above its own code agents. With no ladder or an empty one the ring and slots are as before. A live room reads `client.orchestrators()` at start, every 60 seconds and on an `orchestrator` notice, leaves the main orchestrator out and calls `room.setLadder`; when the client has no `orchestrators()` method or the read fails, it calls `room.setLadder(null)` and the ring stays as it is. A sub keeps its sector arc from its lanes even before its `sub-<id>` agent is in the roster. A replay with `?ladder=demo` sets a fixed ladder of two subs. A wireframe `INTERNET` sphere floats high above the hub centre, over the orchestrator, with a small label under it; its vertices breathe slowly by a seeded noise (`morphOffsets`, pure, tested) and it holds still under reduced motion. A live room reads `client.network?.()` at start, every 30 seconds and on a `network` notice, and listens to the window's `online` and `offline` events, and calls `room.setOnline`; false turns the wireframe and label `#ff2a2a`, the label reads `NO CONNECTION` and the breathing goes on, while a failed read, a client without the method and a replay leave the sphere as it is. A live room reads `client.gitState?.()` at start, every 60 seconds and on a `git` notice and calls `room.setGitState`; a replay, a client without the method and `{ git: false }` draw no tree. The project's git tree sits beneath the root project card as a horizontal timeline, its trunk `GIT_ROOT_GAP` 120 units under the card's bottom, oldest on the left and newest on the right at a step capped at `GIT_MAX_WIDTH` 1400 units, and follows the card as it grows or pops (`cards.bottom`, `setTop`). It keeps the current branch's first parent chain on the hub's axis as the trunk, and leans every other branch out from the commit where it leaves the trunk at its own angle in branch name order below the trunk over the half circle around the x axis, with the leftover commits on the same half circle; it draws one white `LineSegments` for every parent link and one `Points` for every commit with the current head in `#e5ff00`, and puts a small name sprite at each branch tip, at most 12, the current branch first and then the newest. It builds its buffers only when the sha list or the current branch changes, allocates nothing per frame, holds still under reduced motion, and takes no part in the overview fit. An invisible 60 unit box around the whole tree takes the click: a single click opens the inspector on `Source control` without moving the camera, and a double click also holds the camera on the tree. The `machineroom:show-git-tree` window event opens the same panel and holds the camera on the tree as a card hold does; `park()` and `0` return to the overview. A run start pulses the ring white, a run end pulses it green or red, and each slot marker takes its agent's colour, written only when it changes. Under reduced motion the dash ring stops turning, slot markers do not grow, and pulses only change brightness where they end.
- The live roster: a live room re-reads `/api/agents` at start, every 60 seconds and on each `agents` notice, and calls `room.setRoster` when the keys, their order or the set changed (`diffRoster` in `src/live/source.js`, `loadAgents`). The room adds a view, chip, callout and hub slot for a new agent, removes the view, chip, callout and slot of one that is gone, removes its children, and re-spaces the parked lanes without rebuilding the room or moving the camera; if the focused agent is gone, the room forgets it and returns to the overview only when the camera still held or followed it. `takesHubSlot` in `src/engine/hub.js` leaves out the orchestrator, each sub orchestrator and a session reader (a key ending in `-session`, or `sessionReader: true`). A session reader takes no hub slot and draws nothing, chip and callout included, until its first run starts; then it parks outside the ring at `readerPoint` instead of on a slot. A replay and a demo keep the roster they loaded. In a live room the page also reads `client.family?.()` at start, every 60 seconds and on a `family` notice, builds one key per node (a sub's id `X` is `sub-X`, the main is `orchestrator`) and calls `room.setLabels`, so the terminal chip title, the callout title and the HUD agent list row show the owner's label where one is set and the key otherwise, and a child chip reads `PARENT > NAME` with the parent's label; a replay and a client without `family` keep their names.
